US import requirements for fresh produce sit with several agencies at once, and they do not check the same things. Confusing them is the most common reason a first-time importer gets stuck.

This is an orientation, not legal advice. Requirements change, and you should confirm current rules with each agency and with your customs broker before shipping.

## Who does what

**APHIS**, part of USDA, is the plant health authority. It decides whether a given commodity from a given country may enter at all, and under what conditions — treatment, pest-free area status, an import permit. If APHIS does not admit your product from Brazil, nothing else in this article matters.

**FDA** regulates food safety. Its concerns are contamination, adulteration, labelling, and the systems behind them — including prior notice of imported shipments and facility registration.

**CBP** is Customs and Border Protection, which handles entry, classification and duty, and enforces the other agencies' holds at the border.

**PACA** — the Perishable Agricultural Commodities Act — is different in kind. It is a trading rule administered by USDA governing fair conduct in the fresh produce trade, and it is the mechanism behind the statutory trust that protects unpaid sellers.

## Check admissibility first

Before anything else, confirm the specific commodity is admissible from Brazil. APHIS maintains a database of what may be imported from where and under what conditions. Admissibility is **commodity- and origin-specific**: a fruit admissible from one country may not be admissible from another, and conditions differ.

If entry requires a treatment — irradiation, cold treatment, fumigation — that requirement shapes your whole supply chain, because the treatment has to happen somewhere approved, and it has to be documented.

## FSVP is the one people miss

Under FDA's Foreign Supplier Verification Program, the **US importer** carries a legal obligation to verify that their foreign supplier produces food meeting US safety standards. This is not something your Brazilian exporter can do for you.

You will need a named FSVP importer with a US address and a unique facility identifier, and you will need records showing evaluation of the supplier and their hazard controls. FDA does audit this, and it audits the importer, not the exporter.

If you are new to importing, resolve FSVP before you buy fruit. It is the requirement most likely to be discovered late.

## PACA and getting paid

PACA matters commercially rather than at the border. Licensed dealers in the US fresh produce trade operate under rules on prompt payment and fair dealing, and PACA establishes a statutory trust giving unpaid produce sellers a preferential claim over a buyer's assets.

For a foreign exporter, this is meaningful protection. For a US buyer, it is a reason to understand your obligations before a dispute rather than during one.

## A practical order of operations

1. Confirm **APHIS admissibility** for the exact commodity and origin.
2. Establish the **FSVP importer** and the records to support it.
3. Register with **FDA** as required and set up **prior notice** filing.
4. Appoint a **customs broker** and confirm classification and duty.
5. Understand your **PACA** position, whether you need a licence, and what the trust means for you.
6. Only then, review a **draft document set** with your broker before the first container loads.
