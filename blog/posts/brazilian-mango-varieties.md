Mango is Brazil's flagship fresh fruit export, and most of it is a handful of varieties. They are not interchangeable, and the differences show up in exactly the places that matter to a buyer: how the fruit travels, how it looks on a shelf, and how it eats.

## Tommy Atkins

The workhorse. Deep red blush, firm flesh, thick skin, and genuinely excellent shipping and shelf-life characteristics. If a mango programme has to survive a long ocean transit and rough handling, this is why Tommy Atkins exists.

The trade-off is eating quality. It is more fibrous and less aromatic than the alternatives, and consumers who have eaten a good Palmer notice. It sells on appearance.

## Palmer

Elongated, largely red-purple, low fibre, and a markedly better eating experience — sweeter and smoother. It has become the variety of choice for buyers competing on quality rather than price.

It is more demanding in transit and less forgiving of a compromised cold chain. If your cold chain is genuinely continuous, Palmer rewards you. If it is not, Palmer is where that shows up first.

## Keitt

Green at maturity even when fully ripe, which is a merchandising problem and an opportunity in equal measure. Consumers reach for red. But Keitt is large, low-fibre and eats well, and it harvests **later** than the others — which is precisely its value.

Keitt extends a programme past the point where Tommy and Palmer are finished. For a buyer trying to hold a shelf position for one more month, that is the whole argument.

## Kent

Sweet, low fibre, greenish with a red blush. Kent occupies a middle position: better eating than Tommy, more robust than Palmer at its best. It is well regarded in European markets in particular.

## Choosing

| If you need | Consider | Because |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maximum shipping robustness | Tommy Atkins | Thick skin, firm flesh, forgiving |
| Best eating quality | Palmer | Low fibre, sweeter, aromatic |
| To extend the window | Keitt | Later harvest than the others |
| A balance for European retail | Kent | Good eating, reasonable robustness |

## The specification that matters more than the variety

Two containers of the same variety from the same valley can arrive completely differently. What separates them:

- **Maturity at harvest.** Dry matter and brix at picking set the ceiling for eating quality. Nothing downstream raises it.
- **Pulp temperature at loading.** Mango is chilling-sensitive; carried too cold it pits and develops off flavours. See [reefer set-points](/blog/reefer-set-points-for-tropical-fruit/).
- **Post-harvest handling.** Hot water treatment where required, careful grading, and clean packing.
- **Calibre consistency.** A pallet with three calibres in it is a pallet the retailer will argue about.

> Specify the variety, then specify the brix, the calibre and the pulp temperature. The variety alone tells you less than half of what determines whether the programme works.
