GLOBALG.A.P. — what it proves, and what it does not

Certification is a floor, not a guarantee. What the standard actually covers, how to verify a certificate is live, and the questions that tell you more than the logo does.

Almost every retailer buying fresh produce asks for GLOBALG.A.P. certification, and almost every exporter says they have it. Both statements are usually true and neither tells you very much on its own.

What the standard covers

GLOBALG.A.P. is a farm assurance standard. Its Integrated Farm Assurance scope covers, broadly:

  • Food safety and traceability
  • Environmental management, including water and soil
  • Worker health, safety and welfare
  • Plant protection product use and record-keeping

It is audited by accredited third-party certification bodies rather than by the scheme itself. That independence is the point.

What it does not cover

It is not a quality standard. It says nothing about whether the fruit tastes good, sizes well, or arrives sound. A fully certified farm can produce fruit you would not want to sell.

It is also farm-level. It does not, by itself, tell you anything about the packhouse chain of custody, the cold chain after the farm gate, or the exporter's paperwork discipline. Those are separate questions with separate answers.

Verify the certificate, not the claim

This is the part most buyers skip. Every certified operation has a GGN — a GLOBALG.A.P. Number — and the scheme runs a public database where a certificate can be checked.

What to confirm:

  1. The GGN is live, not expired or suspended.
  2. The scope covers the product you are buying. A grower certified for grape is not thereby certified for mango.
  3. The certificate holder is the farm actually supplying you, not a group the exporter is loosely associated with.
  4. The certification type. Option 1 is an individual producer; Option 2 is a producer group with an internal quality management system. Both are valid; they mean different things about how the audit sampled.

Ask for the GGN in writing and check it yourself. It takes a minute and it is the only step in this list that cannot be talked around.

Add-ons and buyer-specific modules

Many buyers require more than the base standard — a social practices add-on such as GRASP, or a retailer-specific module. If your customer requires one, specify it at the tender stage. Retro-fitting an add-on onto a grower mid-programme is slow and sometimes impossible within the season.

The questions that actually differentiate

Once certification is confirmed, these tell you more:

  • Which farms, by name, will supply this programme? An exporter who cannot answer is buying on the open market.
  • What is the residue testing regime, who does it, and can you see recent results?
  • How is traceability maintained from field to carton — what is the smallest unit you can trace back?
  • What happens on a non-conformance? A good answer describes a process. A weak answer describes a hope.

Certification tells you a floor has been met. It does not tell you how far above the floor anyone is standing.
